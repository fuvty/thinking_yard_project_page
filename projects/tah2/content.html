THE METHOD

Learning when to iterate.

The backbone and decider learn together, using the actual gain from another iteration.

Explore the method ↗
01 / REINJECT

Keep the input in the loop.

A learned updater combines the original embedding with the previous hidden state.

02 / SUPERVISE

Learn from iteration gains.

Online, cost-sensitive depth labels teach the decider where extra compute helps.

03 / COMBINE

Use the predictions along the way.

Stopping probabilities weight predictions across the executed iterations.

What changes from TaH to TaH2?+

TaH uses staged training and offline top-1 mismatch labels. TaH2 jointly trains the backbone, updater and decider with lookahead depth supervision: labels track gains on the current backbone. Both training and inference follow the decider’s thresholded decisions. Extended duo-causal attention preserves causality across token positions and iteration depths.

At inference, a token stops when its continuation probability is below 0.5, or when it reaches the depth ceiling. Training-only lookahead is omitted. The updater and decider add fewer than 3% parameters at the studied scales.

THE EVIDENCE

Compute, accuracy,
and latency.

Steeper sequential and parallel test-time scaling, lower loss throughout training, real wall-clock gains in serving, and depth that follows the content.

Qwen3-1.7B · AIME24–26 · avg@32

The 1.7B study averages AIME24–26 accuracy over 32 samples. The 4K–16K fit gains 2.74 versus 1.79 percentage points per compute doubling (+53%). At the 32K evaluation scale, TaH2 gains about 3.4 percentage points at matched compute. At a fixed 16K output cutoff, increasing the depth ceiling from 2 to 8 raises the gain over Standard from 2.8 to 3.9 percentage points, with additional compute.
